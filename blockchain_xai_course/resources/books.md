# Books — The Permanent Shelf

Books worth owning (paper or PDF) for both fields. Listed by track, then priority.

---

## Blockchain — must-own

1. **Bitcoin and Cryptocurrency Technologies** — Narayanan, Bonneau, Felten, Miller, Goldfeder.
   Free PDF. The most rigorous Bitcoin textbook ever written.

2. **Mastering Bitcoin (2nd ed)** — Andreas Antonopoulos.
   Practical reference; chapters on UTXO, scripts, addresses are gold.

3. **Mastering Ethereum** — Antonopoulos & Wood.
   Practical companion for EVM, Solidity, smart contract security.

4. **A Graduate Course in Applied Cryptography** — Dan Boneh & Victor Shoup. *Free PDF.*
   The crypto reference. Read chapters 5, 12, 13, 18.

5. **Introduction to Modern Cryptography (3rd ed)** — Katz & Lindell.
   The standard graduate textbook. Heavier on formal definitions.

6. **Building Bitcoin in C** — Brandon Williams. (If you really want to feel the metal.)

7. **Mastering Blockchain** — Imran Bashir.
   Broad survey including Hyperledger, Corda — useful for enterprise context.

8. **DeFi and the Future of Finance** — Campbell Harvey et al.
   Lecture-book companion; concise.

9. **Radical Markets** — Posner & Weyl.
   Quadratic voting, Harberger taxes — mechanism-design foundation for DAO research.

10. **The Infinite Machine** — Camila Russo.
    Narrative history of Ethereum; great context for protocol decisions.

11. **Programming Bitcoin** — Jimmy Song.
    Build a Bitcoin client from scratch in Python. Highly recommended hands-on.

12. **The Art of Bitcoin** — Various — protocol-level deep dives via Bitcoin Optech newsletter archives.

---

## XAI — must-own

1. **Interpretable Machine Learning** — Christoph Molnar. *Free online.*
   The canonical XAI textbook. Re-read it twice.

2. **Explanatory Model Analysis** — Biecek & Burzykowski. *Free online.*
   Tabular-focused; pairs well with Molnar.

3. **The Book of Why** — Judea Pearl & Dana Mackenzie.
   Causal-inference intuition for anyone touching counterfactuals.

4. **Causal Inference in Statistics: A Primer** — Pearl, Glymour, Jewell.
   Formal but readable.

5. **Causal Inference: What If** — Hernán & Robins. *Free PDF.*
   Epidemiologist's perspective; the standard.

6. **Trustworthy Machine Learning** — Kush Varshney. *Free online.*
   Spans XAI, fairness, robustness, privacy.

7. **Fairness and Machine Learning** — Barocas, Hardt, Narayanan. *Free online.*
   Definitive fairness textbook.

8. **Pattern Recognition and Machine Learning** — Bishop.
   Bayesian baseline; needed to read XAI papers fluently.

9. **Deep Learning** — Goodfellow, Bengio, Courville. *Free online.*
   Reference for the neural-net building blocks XAI methods explain.

10. **Probabilistic Machine Learning: Advanced Topics** — Kevin Murphy.
    Bayesian XAI roots; influence functions, etc.

## Cross-cutting

- **The Mythical Man-Month** — Brooks. Engineering wisdom.
- **High Output Management** — Grove. For when you start collaborating.
- **Designing Data-Intensive Applications** — Kleppmann. Distributed-systems intuition for blockchain.
- **The Elements of Statistical Learning** — Hastie, Tibshirani, Friedman. *Free PDF.*
- **An Introduction to Statistical Learning** — James, Witten, Hastie, Tibshirani. *Free PDF.* Friendlier.
