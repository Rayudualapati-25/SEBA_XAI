\begin{abstract}
In criminal-justice workflows, job role alone is not sufficient to decide
access to a sensitive record. For example, a sub-inspector and a constable may
request the same case file and should not receive the same answer; the same
officer may be permitted for one purpose and denied for another; and some
records must remain closed to one department while another may read them. This
paper proposes SEBA-XAI, an explainable access-governance overlay for record
sharing between agencies. For every request the system evaluates attributes of
the requester, the record, the requested action, the stated purpose and the
operating context, and returns \emph{allow}, \emph{deny} or \emph{escalate}.
It also stores the grounds for the decision: the attributes that were decisive
and the policy version applied. Both are recorded in a tamper-evident audit
trail on a permissioned blockchain that multiple agencies can verify, while
the record remains with the agency holding it. The paper also studies an
attack that such audit trails cannot detect. If an attacker controls the
component that signs audit entries, a decision can be modified and signed
again: the entry passes verification although the stored decision was never
authorised. Since real access logs are not public, evaluation uses a
reproducible synthetic workload over five seeds. The results show that all
integrity-based defences considered detect ordinary tampering but obtain 0.00
detection for this attack, whereas two methods that check the decisions
instead of the signatures obtain 1.00 detection under stronger visibility
assumptions. Integrity and correctness are therefore separate properties, and
tamper detection alone is not sufficient to establish trust in an audit trail.
\end{abstract}
