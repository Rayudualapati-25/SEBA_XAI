\title{SEBA-XAI: Explainable Policy-Aware Audit for Secure Inter-Agency Police Data Access Governance}

\author{Alapati~Venkat~Rayudu}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Inter-agency police data sharing cannot be handled only as a database-sharing
problem. Sensitive record access needs contextual authorization, tamper-evident
audit, and explanations that can be reviewed later. This paper proposes
SEBA-XAI, a secure, explainable, blockchain-audited overlay for CCTNS/ICJS-style
police record access governance. The system combines a layered RBAC/ABAC/PBAC
access-control service, off-chain sensitive-record storage, permissioned
blockchain-style audit commitments, and an explainable decision service for
\emph{allow}, \emph{deny}, and \emph{escalate} decisions. The paper also
studies a validly re-signed compromised-signer threat, where policy output is
corrupted but the audit log still appears internally valid. The evaluation uses
a reproducible synthetic workload because real police access logs are not
publicly available. The current evidence supports a limited claim:
blockchain-style audit, contextual policy re-evaluation, trusted raw-attribute
checking, and explainable log-only drift detection catch different failure modes
under different visibility assumptions. Across five synthetic seeds, the
validly re-signed compromised-signer attack is missed by integrity-only audit
baselines but detected by NS-PI drift and a trusted raw-attribute policy oracle
under their respective assumptions. The work does not claim live
CCTNS/ICJS deployment, real police-record testing, legal compliance, production
security, or crime prediction.
\end{abstract}
